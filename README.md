# Stratego endgame databases with the two-square rule

Distance-to-mate (DTM) endgame databases for Stratego endgames with up to three moving pieces, on the classic
10×10 board with the two lakes, **with the two-square rule (tweeveldenregel) encoded in the state**. The rule's limit
is a parameter, so the effect of the rule on results and lengths can be measured exactly.

|---|---|
| [`src/stratego_egdb.cpp`](src/stratego_egdb.cpp) | generic databases for up to 2 pieces per side, ranks S < L < K < M, two-square rule with limit `-k 1…` or `-k inf`, rank collapsing, retrograde analysis with move counters, OpenMP, full verification |
| [`src/stratego22.cpp`](src/stratego22.cpp) | 2 lieutenants vs 1 and vs 2 sergeants without the two-square rule (4 pieces) |
| [`scripts/table.py`](scripts/table.py) | summary tables from the logs |
| [`scripts/no_capture.py`](scripts/no_capture.py) | red wins with red to move when red cannot attack at once |
| [`scripts/compare.py`](scripts/compare.py) | per limit: wins that only exist with the rule, and wins that disappear with it |
| [`scripts/ls_criterion.py`](scripts/ls_criterion.py) | checks the closed-form L–S criterion against the databases |

The engine works on a fixed-size position (`std::array` per side, no allocation in the hot paths):

* **index**: each group of identical pieces is ranked among the remaining free squares (combinatorial number system),
  mixed radix over the groups; then one digit per side for the restriction state; side to move is the last digit
  (`index * 2 + stm`), one byte per entry in a single `std::vector<std::uint8_t>`;
* **retrograde analysis**: an initial pass resolves terminal states and states whose moves all leave the database
  (captures go to smaller databases), and counts the moves that stay inside; then wins and losses are propagated by
  level with bucket queues; parents are generated by unmoves, using the restriction state to reconstruct the
  previous restriction (count < k − 1: it was the same piece one step earlier; count = k − 1: unrestricted, another
  direction, or the other piece);
* **verification**: every state is recomputed from its children afterwards (Bellman check); all runs report 0 errors;
* **rank collapsing**: only comparisons between red and blue matter, so the ranks are renumbered to the fewest levels
  that keep every comparison, through a precomputed table (K vs L+S = L vs 2S, 2L vs L = 2S vs S, K+L vs L = L+S vs S).

## Rules and conventions

* Pieces move one square orthogonally, never into a lake. In an attack the higher rank wins; equal ranks are both removed.
* A side without pieces has lost (both without pieces: draw). The side to move without a legal move loses, unless
  the opponent could not move either (were it their turn): then it is a draw.
* **Two-square rule with limit k**: a piece may make at most k consecutive moves between the same two squares.
  State per side: at most one restricted piece with (square, direction back, count 0…k−1). Moving piece p in
  direction e: if it is the restricted piece moving in its restricted direction (needs count > 0), the new state is
  (p, reverse e, count − 1), otherwise (p, reverse e, k − 1). When the restricted piece disappears, its side is free.
  Unrestricted states are reachable from larger endgames, so they are part of every database. The default k = 3
  allows A→B→A→B and then forbids B→A.
* **Colours**: red is the side with the highest piece, on equal highest pieces the side with more pieces; the other
  side is blue.
* **DTM in plies**, one byte per state: odd n = win in n plies for the side to move, even n = loss in n plies
  (0 = no move or no pieces), 254 = draw, 253 = unreachable restriction state.
* Percentages are over the states **in which nobody is restricted**, the only states that exist for every limit; the
  longest win is over all states.
* Diagrams: red pieces in capitals, blue in lower case, `~` is a lake.

So the 2-vs-1 endgames with a piece at least as strong are the analogue of KQ–K / KR–K in chess: always won, with a
straightforward method (Vincent de Boer's enclosing method: enclose each moved piece in turn), at most 26 moves.

Observations:

* The won 2-vs-1 endgames do not depend on the rule at all: 100% and 52 plies for every k.
* **k = 1 is a different game**: a piece may never step straight back. L–S and L–SS more than double in length
  (65 instead of 29 plies), and in L–SS 432 wins disappear: the lieutenant that captured cannot step back and loses the
  opposition in the remaining L–S. A restriction of the attacker can hurt the attacker.
* For k ≥ 2 the rule only adds wins here. In LS–L that is a fixed set of 416 (red to move) and 352 (blue to move)
  states that are drawn without the rule because blue can oscillate forever, the same for every k ≥ 2. Their length
  grows with k up to k = 4 (longest 51, 55, 59, 59 for k = 2…5), after which the rule no longer binds.
* In the quiet positions (nobody restricted, nobody can attack) L–SS is always a draw, for every k and either side to
  move: all its wins come from an immediate capture or from a restricted sergeant.
* For a first overall picture of an endgame, k = ∞ is the natural first run: the state space is smallest (LS–L:
  0.75 M states per side instead of 245 M for k = 3; the factor is (1 + 4k·n_red)(1 + 4k·n_blue)), and the verdicts
  differ only in the rule-dependent corner cases.

## L–S in closed form: opposition and the lake ring

For k = ∞ and every k ≥ 2, on all 8,372 placements and for both sides to move (zero deviations, checked by
[`scripts/ls_criterion.py`](scripts/ls_criterion.py)): **red wins L–S if and only if**

1. **L has the opposition**: red to move and odd distance, or blue to move and even distance (on this board simply the
   colour of the squares), and
2. **S cannot reach the ring around a lake at least 5 plies before L**. The rings are the 12 squares around each lake
   (b4–e4, b7–e7, b5, b6, e5, e6 and f4–i4, f7–i7, f5, f6, i5, i6); the side to move reaches a square at distance d
   after 2d − 1 plies, the other side after 2d.

Why 5: with the opposition at L the lead can only be 1, 5, 9, … plies (red to move: 2Δ − 1 with Δ odd; blue to move:
2Δ + 1 with Δ even, Δ the difference in distance), so "at least 5" just means "more than 1". With a lead of 1 ply
L arrives on the ring square right after S and captures it there. With a larger lead S is on the 12-cycle around the
lake with L at least two squares away, and a single pursuer never catches a piece that runs around a cycle (the cop
number of a cycle is 2). Running around the lake never repeats two squares, so the rule is irrelevant for k ≥ 2.
For k = 1 there are 80 (red to move) and 100 (blue to move) extra wins.

## LS–L: the edge sergeant and the pendulum

The 416 LS–L positions (red to move, nobody restricted) that are drawn without the rule and won with every k ≥ 2 have
one theme: a red sergeant on the edge under perpetual attack. Example, red L e5, S e1, blue L f2, red to move:

```
    +---------------------+
 10 | . . . . . . . . . . |
  9 | . . . . . . . . . . |
  8 | . . . . . . . . . . |
  7 | . . . . . . . . . . |
  6 | . . ~ ~ . . ~ ~ . . |
  5 | . . ~ ~ L . ~ ~ . . |
  4 | . . . . . . . . . . |
  3 | . . . . . . . . . . |
  2 | . . . . . l . . . . |
  1 | . . . . S . . . . . |
    +---------------------+
      a b c d e f g h i j
```

| k | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | ∞ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| result | win 25 | win 29 | win 29 | win 33 | win 33 | draw |

Main line for k = 3: 1. Le5-e4 Lf2-e2 2. Se1-f1 Le2-f2 3. Sf1-e1 Lf2-f1 4. Se1-e2 Lf1-f2 5. Se2-e3 Lf2-e2
6. Se3-f3 Le2-f2 7. Sf3-f4 Lf2-f3 8. Sf4-f5 Lf3-g3 9. Le4-f4 Lg3-h3 10. Lf4-g4 Lh3-i3 11. Lg4-h4 Li3-j3
12. Lh4-i4 Lj3-j2 13. Li4-i3 Lj2-j1 14. Li3-i2 Lj1-j2 15. Li2xj2 (exchange), and the lone sergeant wins.

* **Without the rule**: with the blue lieutenant directly above it, the sergeant on the edge can only move sideways;
  running along the edge ends in the corner, so it shuttles e1↔f1 while blue shuttles e2↔f2. Red has to move the
  sergeant every move and never gets a tempo for the lieutenant: a perpetual attack, draw.
* **With the rule, whoever started the pendulum has to stop first.** Blue starts it with the attacking move
  1…Lf2-e2, half a move before red, so blue's count runs out first and blue has to give up the attack from above
  (3…Lf2-f1). The sergeant escapes via e2-e3-f3-f4-f5 towards its own lieutenant, which then drives the blue
  lieutenant into the corner with the opposition and forces the exchange.
* The lengths step by 4 plies per two units of k (29, 29, 33, 33): both pendulums get longer with k. It is tempo
  counting as in a draughts fortress, but here the rule is the clock, not the board.

Reproduce with `./build/stratego_egdb -k 3 -load L LS -pos Le5,Se1 Lf2 r` after a run with `-k 3`.

## L–SS: the corner curiosities

Without restrictions blue to move never loses L–SS (0 of 376,740 placements, every k), and red to move only wins by
an immediate capture. With k = 3 there are exactly 88 lost states with blue to move: 8 board patterns (one corner pattern,
in the four corners and in both orientations) × 11 red restriction states, all with a **restricted blue sergeant in the corner**
([list](results/l-ss_lost_blue_to_move_k3.txt)), e.g. red L c2, blue S a1 b1, a1 may no longer go north:

```
    +---------------------+
  3 | . . . . . . . . . . |
  2 | . . L . . . . . . . |
  1 | s s . . . . . . . . |
    +---------------------+
      a b c d e f g h i j
```

Every blue move walks into the lieutenant (loss in 22 plies). Positions one red move away are won as well, e.g. red
L b4 vs blue S a1 a2 with a1 restricted to the east: 1. Lb4-b3 wins in 23 plies.

[`src/stratego22.cpp`](src/stratego22.cpp), without the two-square rule: 2L–1S is won everywhere in at most 52 plies,
2L–2S is won everywhere in at most 115 plies with red to move (L a1 b1 vs S f1 i1) and 116 with blue to move
(L a1 b1 vs S f1 h1); see [the log](results/stratego22.log). Lower pieces never protect each other.

## Reproducing everything

```sh
cmake -B build -DCMAKE_CXX_COMPILER=g++-14
cmake --build build -j
mkdir -p dump
for k in inf 1 2 3 4 5; do
    ./build/stratego_egdb -k $k L S LL S L SS LL L L LS KL L > results/k$k.log
done
python3 scripts/table.py results/k1.log results/k2.log results/k3.log results/k4.log results/k5.log results/kinf.log
python3 scripts/no_capture.py dump 1 2 3 4 5 inf
python3 scripts/compare.py dump 1 2 3 4 5
python3 scripts/ls_criterion.py dump inf 1 2 3 4 5
./build/stratego_egdb -k 3 -load L LS -pos Le5,Se1 Lf2 r   # one position from the stored databases
./build/stratego22 > results/stratego22.log
```

A material pair is given as red and blue ranks (`L S`, `KL L`, …); it is canonicalised and its sub-databases are
built first. The pairs above produce the seven canonical databases. Timings on 4 cores: k = ∞ about 1 s in total,
k = 3 about 3.5 minutes, k = 5 about 9 minutes. The databases are written to `dump/` (about 10 GB for all limits,
not in this repository); the logs are in [`results/`](results).
